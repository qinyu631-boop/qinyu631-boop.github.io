## Working Papers

### Host Country Language and School Integration of Immigrant Students

*With Michael Vlassopoulos and Jackline Wahba*  
**R&R — Journal of Population Economics**

This paper studies how host-country language proficiency shapes immigrant students’ school integration. Stronger language skills improve social inclusion and reduce exposure to bullying, highlighting the importance of language support for immigrant children’s wellbeing and participation at school.

### Breathing Easy, Retiring Early? Effects of Air Pollution on Retirement Age Expectations: Evidence from China

*With Dingzhi Chen*  
Working Paper

This paper examines how air pollution shapes retirement expectations in China. Higher pollution leads workers to expect earlier retirement, particularly among rural workers, women, and those in physically demanding jobs, suggesting that environmental risks influence labour supply decisions well before retirement occurs.

### Following Cleaner Air: Air Pollution and Destination Choice Across Mobility and Migration in China

*With Dingzhi Chen*  
Working Paper

This paper examines how air pollution influences destination choice and migration in China. Pollution discourages travel planning, actual mobility, and long-term migration, with a stronger effect on planning than on realised movement, suggesting that pollution avoidance begins before people travel.

### Green Remittances: How Migration Shapes Green Behaviours in Rural China

*With Dingzhi Chen and Fengtian Yang*  
Working Paper

This paper studies how migration shapes environmental behaviour in rural China. Current migration can weaken everyday green practices through household separation, while return migration improves recycling and waste sorting, suggesting that migrants bring home environmental norms and skills acquired in urban areas.

### When Reading is Hard: Reading Proficiency and Generative AI Use among Immigrant Students

*With Dingzhi Chen*  
Working Paper

This paper examines how language proficiency shapes generative AI use among immigrant students. Students with weaker reading skills use AI more for summarising, preliminary research, and writing, suggesting that AI can help compensate for language constraints in specific tasks rather than general learning.

### When Models Disagree: Measuring Macroeconomic and Financial Uncertainty

*With Fengtian Yang and Dingzhi Chen*  
Working Paper

This paper develops measures of macroeconomic and financial uncertainty based on disagreement across forecasting models. Model disagreement predicts subsequent forecast errors beyond uncertainty within individual models, offering a distinct view of the risks surrounding economic and financial forecasts.

### AI Washing and Recruitment Strategies: Evidence from China

*With Dingzhi Chen*  
Working Paper

This paper investigates whether firms’ AI-related claims align with their underlying capabilities and how gaps between the two shape recruitment in China. It examines hiring intensity, occupational composition, skill requirements, and offered wages to understand the labour market implications of corporate AI washing.

### When the Air Gets Dirty: Recruitment Responses in China’s Listed Companies

*With Dingzhi Chen*  
Working Paper

This paper studies how listed firms in China adjust recruitment when air pollution rises. Firms reduce advertised wages for higher-skilled positions and expand hiring for non-managerial roles, suggesting that environmental stress can reshape labour demand and the composition of recruitment.
