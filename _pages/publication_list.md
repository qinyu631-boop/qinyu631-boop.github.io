## Revise & Resubmit

### Host Country Language and School Integration of Immigrant Students

*With Michael Vlassopoulos and Jackline Wahba*

**R&R at Journal of Population Economics**

Using PISA data from 16 OECD countries and an instrumental-variable strategy based on age at arrival and linguistic distance, we estimate the causal impact of host-country language proficiency on immigrant students’ school integration. A one-standard-deviation increase in reading proficiency significantly improves social integration and reduces bullying exposure.

## Job Market Paper

### Breathing Easy, Retiring Early? Effects of Air Pollution on Retirement Age Expectations: Evidence from China

*With Dingzhi Chen*

**Under Review at Journal of Environmental Economics and Management**

Using longitudinal survey data merged with satellite-derived pollution measures, this paper examines how deteriorating air quality shapes forward-looking labour supply decisions. The results show that higher pollution significantly lowers expected retirement age, particularly among rural workers, women, and individuals in physically demanding jobs. Additional evidence suggests that health insurance coverage, financial resources, and green infrastructure mediate the response, indicating that perceived health risks and institutional protection jointly shape labour supply expectations.

## Working Papers

### Following Cleaner Air: Air Pollution and Destination Choice Across Mobility and Migration in China

*With Dingzhi Chen*

**Invited Resubmission at Regional Science and Urban Economics**

This paper examines how air pollution shapes destination choice using high-frequency inter-city mobility data from the Gaode Migration Big Data Platform and bilateral migration data from the Chinese Population Census. Exploiting destination–origin thermal inversion differences as an instrument for PM2.5 exposure, we show that pollution significantly reduces destination planning, realized mobility, and long-run migration, with stronger effects on planning than on realized mobility. The findings suggest that pollution avoidance begins before travel occurs and that studies based only on realized mobility underestimate behavioural responses to environmental risk.

### Green Remittances: How Migration Shapes Green Behaviours in Rural China

*With Dingzhi Chen and Fengtian Yang*

**Submitted to World Development**

This paper studies how migration influences the environmental behaviour of rural households. While current migration can weaken daily pro-environmental practices through household fragmentation, return migration improves recycling and waste sorting, consistent with the transmission of environmental norms and skills acquired in urban areas. Social capital and local green infrastructure strengthen these relationships.

### When Reading is Hard: Reading Proficiency and Generative AI Use among Immigrant Students

*With Dingzhi Chen*

Using PISA 2025 data on first-generation immigrant students across OECD countries, this paper studies how language proficiency shapes the adoption and use of generative AI. Exploiting variation in age at arrival and linguistic distance to instrument reading proficiency, we find that weaker language proficiency increases AI use for summarising, preliminary research, and writing, while having little effect on its use for general learning. The findings suggest that generative AI can serve as a task-specific substitute for constrained human capital, with adoption driven not only by technological capability but also by the need to overcome skill limitations.

### When Models Disagree: Measuring Macroeconomic and Financial Uncertainty

*With Fengtian Yang and Dingzhi Chen*

This paper develops new measures of macroeconomic and financial uncertainty by combining forecasts from four heterogeneous model families, including factor-augmented, regularized, and neural-network approaches. Using an expanding-window pseudo-out-of-sample framework, we decompose predictive risk into within-model uncertainty and cross-model disagreement. The latter predicts subsequent forecast errors beyond within-model variance and exhibits distinct cyclical dynamics, providing a new measure of model uncertainty in macroeconomic and financial forecasting.

## Manuscripts in Preparation

### AI Washing and Recruitment Strategies: Evidence from China

*With Dingzhi Chen*

Using approximately seven million online job postings matched to publicly listed firms in China, this paper examines how corporate AI washing shapes firms’ recruitment strategies. We investigate whether discrepancies between firms’ AI-related claims and their underlying AI capabilities translate into changes in labour demand, including hiring intensity, occupational composition, skill requirements, and offered wages.

### When the Air Gets Dirty: Recruitment Responses in China’s Listed Companies

*With Dingzhi Chen*

Drawing on millions of online job postings combined with firm- and city-level environmental data, this paper investigates how recruitment strategies adjust when pollution rises. Firms reduce advertised wages for higher-skilled positions and expand hiring for non-managerial roles, consistent with shifting labour demand under environmental stress.
